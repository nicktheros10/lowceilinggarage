How to Measure Your Garage for Overhead Storage

Five minutes with a tape measure prevents buying a rack that doesn’t fit. These are the measurements that matter.

1. Floor-to-Ceiling Height

Measure in several spots. Ceilings are rarely perfectly flat. Use the lowest number. Note any beams, pipes, or lights that reduce usable height.

2. Garage Door Clearance (Door Fully Open)

Open the door all the way and measure from the floor to the lowest point of the tracks and opener rail. The rack platform and stored items must clear this path. Low-headroom tracks can change this number significantly.

3. Ceiling Joist Spacing and Direction

Most residential joists are 16" or 24" on center. Note the direction they run. Overhead racks must be mounted into solid joists, not just drywall. Use a stud finder and confirm with a small pilot hole if needed.

4. Vehicle Height + Buffer

Measure the roof height of the tallest vehicle that will park under or near the rack. Add 6–12 inches of buffer for comfort and any roof racks or cargo.

5. Tallest Stored Item

Measure the tallest bin or item you plan to put on the rack. This number goes into the drop math below.

Run the Drop Math

Ceiling height − Minimum drop = Platform height
Platform height − Tallest bin height ≈ Walking clearance

A practical target is roughly 6 feet or more of clearance under a loaded platform for comfortable walking. If the numbers are tight, a smaller footprint rack or wall shelving is usually the better choice.

Once you have these numbers, compare them against the options in the overhead racks guide. If overhead doesn’t work, start with narrow wall shelving instead.

Common Mistakes

  • Measuring only at the door opening instead of the main ceiling.
  • Ignoring the garage door tracks when the door is fully open.
  • Assuming “adjustable” means the rack can go as high as you want (check the minimum drop).
  • Not confirming joist location and condition before buying.
